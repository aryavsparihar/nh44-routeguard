## 3 Key design decisions

### Decision first

- The advisor answers in one word (SEND, HOLD, RAIL, DIVERT) at 22 px bold with an icon, then one sentence of why. A grower decides before loading, so the answer comes before the detail.
- Every decision has a different icon shape (arrow, pause bars, train, branching arrow), so it reads without colour.

### Colour and contrast

- The base palette is from our Round 1 deck: terracotta, teal, ochre and navy on a warm canvas (`#FDF8F0`).
- We checked every text pair with the WCAG 2.1 formula. All text is at least 4.5:1. Decision banners and badges are at least 7:1 (AAA) because they are read in bright sun.
- Three base colours failed for text, so we added darker "ink" versions: `brand.terracotta.ink #A9481F`, `brand.ochre.ink #7A5A12`, `risk.medium.map #9A6E1F`. White on ochre is only 3.24:1, so HOLD never puts white text on ochre.
- Risk level is never shown by colour alone: the word is always there, medium cells have stripes, high cells have cross-hatching, and badges carry one or two triangles.

### Touch and type

- Every touch target is at least 48 × 48 px (the hazard buttons are 112 px tall) for cold hands and gloves.
- Body text 14 px, form fields 16 px (so phones do not zoom), labels 12 px uppercase only.
- Fonts: Merriweather for titles (warm, readable), Inter for the interface, JetBrains Mono for numbers so scores line up.

### Offline first

- The phone keeps a text summary of the latest scores, under 5 KB, so it loads on 2G. The prototype shows the real size of that summary.
- Reports made with no signal wait in IndexedDB on the phone (store D7 in the DFD) and send by themselves when the phone is back online. The banner always says how many are waiting.
- If scores are more than 2 h old, the banner turns ochre and says so. We never show old scores as new.

### Fewest steps

- Report a hazard in 3 taps: what (4 big buttons), where (GPS fills it in), send.
- Get advice in 1 form with defaults, then 1 tap to commit the truck to the agent board.
- The police override is one form: zone, status, cause, publish.

### Trust

- Every score shows its confidence (low, medium, high) and a range, not one exact number.
- Road reports are shown by source: police official (solid navy), verified driver (solid green), unconfirmed driver (dashed grey). A driver report stays unconfirmed until police or a second driver confirms it.

## 4 Facts we corrected while building Round 2

- **Replay caveat.** The replay runs our model on archived observed weather from Open-Meteo, not the forecast that existed on that day. So it shows what our rules would say with good inputs. The real backtest on forecasts is planned before the final.
- **Lead time.** On the 24 Aug run, zones 3 to 5 are medium now and already high at +12 h and +24 h. That is about a day of warning before the closure, on observed weather.

## 5 Questions judges may ask

#### Why a PWA and not a native app?

One codebase for phone and desktop, no Play Store install, it works on old Android phones, and a service worker gives us offline mode. Drivers can add it to the home screen from a link in a WhatsApp group.

#### Why hexagons on the map?

H3 res-8 hexagons (about 0.7 km² each) give every part of the corridor the same size cell, so a long zone does not look riskier just because it is bigger. In the prototype the cells are drawn along the route; the app uses our corridor file. The zone buttons under the map repeat it for screen readers and one-handed use.

#### Why not just use Google Maps traffic?

Google shows the jam after the road closes. We forecast the closure 24 to 48 h ahead and tell the grower what to do before the truck is loaded. Slide 5 of Round 1 compares 6 existing tools.

#### What if the forecast is wrong?

We show confidence and a range, never one exact number. Police overrides beat the model. And every advice card says why, so the user can judge. At the final we will report lead time, hit rate and false alarms per month from the backtest.

#### How do you keep colour-blind users safe?

Level is always a word, plus a pattern (stripes for medium, cross-hatch for high), plus triangles on badges. Decisions have different icon shapes. No information is carried by colour alone.

#### How did you check accessibility?

`verify_round2_all.py` computes every contrast ratio, measures every button in every prototype state (21 states) for 48 px, checks font sizes, and checks that the page does not scroll sideways at 390 px. Screenshots of each state are saved for review.

#### Explain your DFD

Level 0 has one process (RouteGuard) and 5 entities: Open-Meteo, grower or trader, transport agent, truck driver, and traffic police. Level 1 opens it into 5 processes: P1.0 hourly risk scoring, P2.0 dispatch advisor and priority scorer, P3.0 hazard report intake and verification, P4.0 shipment board, P5.0 offline cache sync and push alerts, and 7 stores, D1 to D7. Solid lines run today; dashed ones are planned. Every Level 0 flow appears on Level 1 with the same name (balancing). Stores only connect to processes, and every store has data going in and out. Flows are named with nouns, never verbs.

#### Why is the phone's IndexedDB a data store?

Because the offline queue holds data between two processes over time: P3 writes a report into it when there is no signal, and reads it back to send later. That is what a store is in a DFD.

#### Why does the police override set confidence to High?

An official closure is a fact, not a forecast. When TCU publishes, the zone shows high risk now with high confidence, the reason says "TCU official", and drivers in that zone get a push alert.

#### How is the shipment board ranked?

Priority = spoilage × load value. Spoilage is days since picking divided by the shelf life of that variety; value is tonnes × price. So scarce rail and CA slots go to the lots that would lose the most first, not first-come. The shelf-life and price numbers in the prototype are placeholders.

#### Why RAIL only for some lots?

Rail capacity is small (800 to 1,000 MT a day in Sep 2025 against about 13,700 MT leaving the valley [4]). We suggest it only when a zone stays high at +24 h and +48 h, the destination is outside the valley, and the fruit has used at least half its shelf life.

#### Why Mughal Road only for light trucks, May to November?

Mughal Road is closed in winter and is not suited to heavy multi-axle trucks. So DIVERT by Mughal Road is only offered to light 6-wheelers in that season.
